# Academic Writing Rules

## Hierarchy
synthesis > summary · argument > enumeration · evidence > rhetoric ·
critical > descriptive · conditional > absolute.

## Paragraph default moves (choose archetype from benchmark patterns)
Claim → multi-source evidence → comparison/contrast → interpretation →
geographic explanation (only when triggered) → boundary/limitation → implication
→ transition.

Topic sentences carry the proposition; citations never substitute for it.

## Sentence-level bans
- Template openers stacked ("With the rapid development…", "has attracted
  increasing attention…").
- Trend verbs without corpus support ("growing interest" needs evidence of growth).
- Single-citation over-broad claims.
- Weasel quantifiers ("many studies") without a countable anchor in the matrix.
- Decorative spatial jargon (see geography-reasoning-rules.md).

## Structure hygiene
- One section = one job; headings assert content where journal style allows.
- Transitions state logical relations (because/however/at scale), not topic shifts.
- Front-load conclusions; qualifications follow, not hide, them.
- Numbers: exact units; ranges honest; no creative rounding.

## Citation placement (priors from benchmark_corpus/citation-patterns.md)
- Factual claim → sentence-final cluster of independent works.
- Method attribution → author-narrative or parenthetical at method mention.
- Controversy → both positions cited inside the contrast sentence.
- Gap statement → cite the limitation source(s) that justify it.

## Drafting discipline
- Placeholders `<CITE …>` until content freeze; then resolve once.
- Never draft around an unavailable full text (gate blocks those papers anyway).
- After any big rewrite run `scripts/phrase_overlap_check.py`.
